Three drinks · One window

Feel Great, step by step

That is the whole system. No diet list, no guilt trip — just a gentle rhythm you repeat every day, with love, plus a way to track it.

1. Morning, before eating — Unimate

Start the day with Unimate, a purified yerba mate drink. Unicity makes it with a 5-step cleaning and concentrating process they call BioSelect. It is presented for energy, focus, mood, and appetite support, and for helping you stay in a fast.

Unimate is described as rich in natural plant compounds from yerba mate. Unicity says it is much stronger than regular yerba mate or coffee — that is their claim, not something you can check from a graph on this site.

Regular Unimate contains caffeine, about a cup of premium coffee per serving as presented. Decaf Unimate is under 15 mg per serving, for evening use or caffeine-sensitive people.

2. Around lunch — Unimate again

A second Unimate around lunch, or as an afternoon pick-me-up, keeps the same routine going. Some materials also mention a third, usually decaffeinated, in the evening for cravings. The tracker most people use ticks first Unimate, second Unimate, Balance, first meal, last meal, and fast time each day.

3. Biggest meal — Balance

Take Balance with your biggest meal, most often dinner. It is a fiber drink you take before your biggest meal. The fiber turns into a gel inside you, not in the cup, which slows down how fast food turns into sugar in your blood, and helps you feel full and supports blood sugar that is already in a healthy range.

Mix it and drink it right away, powder into water, with a shaker or frother — it is designed to thicken in the stomach, not in the cup. Always follow the pack label for mixing, because Unicity's own materials give different volumes in different documents, and the label is the current instruction.

If you take medication, ask your provider about timing around a fiber drink. Unicity's own materials disagree on the exact hour gap, so this site does not publish one — your provider's answer wins.

Balance Apple — the upgrade version

Balance Apple is presented as the upgraded Balance: a mix of 7 different fibers, plus natural compounds from apples and apple cider vinegar, about 5 g fiber per serving versus about 4 g for original Balance, with added psyllium (a fiber from a plant seed husk) and a fiber from oats, in Natural Apple flavour only. Original Balance comes in Orange and Mixed Berry.

This site presents Balance Apple as an alternative alongside original Balance, not as a forced replacement — Unicity has not settled that question in the materials reviewed here.

4. After dinner — the window closes

Stop eating after dinner, and keep the break until the next day. Most people using this routine use around a 16-hour fast and an 8-hour eating window — for example, eating roughly noon to 8 p.m. Some Unicity materials are looser, at 14–16 hours, and mention about 4 hours between daytime meals.

Missing the fast length on one day is presented in the materials as not losing everything — keep going, and avoid missing two days in a row. Missing a dose: just resume at the next scheduled use.

What is in the pack?

The Feel Great System pack, as described in Unicity's booklet, is one 30-count box of Balance and two 30-count boxes of Unimate — a month of the routine above.

Trackers in Unicity's materials run 31 days, with day 1 vs day 31 measurements only: weight, blood pressure, HbA1c (a 3-month average blood sugar test), and waist, plus notes. There is no food diary, calorie target, or exercise log built into those tools. A Feel Great Fasting app is also offered as a tracking route.

Who should talk to a provider first?

These products are presented for healthy adults. If you are pregnant, nursing, on medication, or have a medical condition, consult a healthcare provider before use. Unicity's clinician materials also flag time-restricted eating as not suiting everyone, including people with serious diabetes, people on medicines that lower blood sugar, underweight people, endurance athletes, people with a history of eating disorders, and people who are pregnant or breastfeeding.

New fiber can cause temporary bloating, gas, loose stools, or constipation. The clinician guidance is to start gradually, stay hydrated, and expect symptoms to ease as the gut adjusts.
